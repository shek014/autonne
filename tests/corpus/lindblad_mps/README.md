# MPS two-site blocks, captured from a running simulation

These are the matrices a matrix-product-state simulator actually hands its SVD,
recorded at the point they are formed. They are not generated by
`tools/make_corpus.py` and cannot be regenerated by it, which is why they sit in
their own directory rather than beside the generated corpus.

## Where they come from

One 24-qubit brickwork circuit of random SU(4) blocks, applied to alternating
bond parities, at a bond dimension cap of 64. The simulator was built with a
compile-time option that dumps each two-site block on its way into the
factorisation. 276 splits, 19 distinct shapes, one representative matrix per
shape.

Row-major, because that is how the simulator stores the block. The reader
accepts either order, and nothing here was transposed on the way out.

## The shape distribution

The frequency matters as much as the shapes. A benchmark that runs five sizes
equally is not weighted like the workload:

```text
rows  cols  count        rows  cols  count
   2     2     36          32     8      6
   2     8      9          32    32     24
   4     4     33          32   128      3
   4    16      9          64    16      6
   8     2      9          64    64     21
   8     8     30          64   128      3
   8    32      6         128    32      3
  16     4      9         128    64      3
  16    16     27         128   128     33
  16    64      6
```

276 splits over 19 shapes. Squares account for 204 of them, or 74%. The
saturated 128x128 case alone is 33 splits, 12% of the run.

Two structural facts, both consequences of how the blocks are formed rather
than of this particular circuit:

- **Every count is a multiple of three.** A three-CX SU(4) block performs three
  splits on the same site pair, and a block's dimensions are
  `2*bond_left x 2*bond_right`, which the block does not change. So the three
  splits within a block are always identically shaped.
- **Those three are strictly ordered.** Each split writes the tensors the next
  one contracts, so the repetition inside a block is serialisation and not an
  opportunity. Independent same-shaped work exists between blocks in a layer,
  where a brickwork applies gates to disjoint site pairs, not within one.

The square counts fall in an exact arithmetic progression, 36 down to 21 by
threes from 2x2 to 64x64, and then 128x128 breaks it by returning to 33. That
is the growth phase against the saturated phase: once the cap binds, bonds stop
growing and sit at 64.

## Not wired into anything

No test lists these and no benchmark reads them. Whether they belong in either
is a decision for this project, and the data is more useful now than a guess
about how to consume it would be.
